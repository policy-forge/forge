# Encryption and Key Management Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines the requirements for establishing encryption implementation and management requirements related to the Organization's computer and communications systems infrastructure.

## Scope

This policy applies to all corporate or third-party data that the Organization creates, manages, or processes, either at rest or in transit.

## Policy

### Authorization

**Encryption Process Approval** — Encryption processes SHALL NOT be used for the Organization's information unless the processes are approved by the CISO or their delegate.

**Exceptions** — Data that would ordinarily be a candidate for encryption may be stored in an unencrypted form only where alternate protections, approved by senior IT management, are used. Deviation requests SHOULD be processed through the standard risk acceptance or exception process.

### Algorithms

**Standard Algorithms** — If encryption is used, government-approved standard algorithms and standard implementations SHALL be consistently employed.

**Publicly Evaluated Algorithms** — Every general-purpose encryption algorithm used to protect production information SHALL be open (publicly disclosed) and SHALL have been evaluated by cryptography experts.

**Asymmetric Algorithms** — The use of RSA is deprecated; new RSA key pairs SHOULD NOT be generated unless a technical limitation prevents the use of Elliptic Curve (EC) cryptography. Known insecure curves SHALL NOT be used. For algorithms that use a hash, SHA-1 is deprecated and new cryptographic materials SHALL NOT be created using SHA-1.

### Requirements

**Key Length — Symmetric** — Production systems employing symmetric algorithms (such as AES) SHALL have a key length of at least 128 bits. User-chosen encryption keys SHALL NOT be shorter than 128 bits.

**Key Length — Asymmetric** — Asymmetric algorithms SHALL employ a key length that provides a comparable level of security as determined by the Information Security Department.

**Key Separation** — If both encryption and digital signatures are used, cryptographically unrelated keys SHALL be used for each.

**Messaging Encryption** — All sensitive information, including passwords and intellectual property, SHALL be encrypted when transmitted through electronic messaging systems.

**Transfer Encryption** — Information sent to another datastore SHALL use acceptable encrypted transport methods. Exceptions may be allowed for publicly available resources.

**Storage Encryption** — Datastores built into mobile computing devices that can travel outside the Organization's protected perimeters SHALL be encrypted at all times.

### Key Management

**Key Management Systems** — Encryption systems for data at rest in production systems SHALL be designed such that no single person has full knowledge of any single encryption key, through the use of an approved Key Management System (KMS).

**Delegation** — Key management responsibility SHALL be delegated only to a party who has passed a background check, passed an operational security audit, and signed a confidentiality agreement.

**Separate Transmission** — If keys are transmitted in a readable form, the protected information SHALL be transmitted over a different communication channel than the keys.

**Automated Key Management** — Whenever commercially available, the Organization SHALL employ automated key management processes rather than manual processes.

**Master Key Access** — At least two trusted and authorized people SHALL have access to the encryption master keys used to protect production information.

**Compromised Keys** — Encryption keys that have been compromised SHALL immediately be revoked retroactively to the last time they were known to be safe. Data SHOULD then be re-encrypted with a new key.

**Key Substitution Prevention** — Key management procedures SHALL prevent the unauthorized substitution of encryption keys.

### Key Generation

**Key Rotation** — Keys used for encrypting data SHALL be changed on a periodic basis. All encryption keys SHALL have a stated life and be changed on or before expiration.

**Key Generation Quality** — Encryption keys SHALL be generated by means that are not practically discernible by an adversary and that yield keys difficult to guess.

**Key Generation Material Security** — Materials to develop encryption keys SHALL be kept locked when not in use. When no longer needed, they SHALL be destroyed by approved methods.

### Key Security

**Key Disclosure** — Encryption keys are highly confidential and access SHALL be strictly limited to those with a need-to-know.

**Key Storage** — Keys employed by end users SHALL be stored in tamper-resistant hardware devices. Non-personal cryptographic keys SHOULD be stored in a secure store such as a KMS, hardware security module (HSM), or virtual HSM.

**Key Secrecy Duration** — The secrecy of any encryption key SHALL be maintained until all protected information is no longer considered confidential.

**Key Storage Media** — Encryption keys and related keying materials SHALL NOT be stored on the same storage media as the data they protect in unencrypted form.

**Key Backup** — Encryption keys used to conceal backup data SHALL themselves be backed up with security measures comparable to or more stringent than the associated data.

**Key Protection Controls** — Encryption keys SHALL be protected from unauthorized disclosure through technical controls such as encryption under a separate key and use of tamper-resistant hardware.

**Key Escrow Restrictions** — Keys used for digital signatures, digital certificates, and user authentication SHALL NOT be included in a key escrow arrangement.

### Monitoring

**Encryption Monitoring** — Where systems support such mechanisms, ways to highlight unencrypted data SHOULD be employed so that the validity of such findings can be assessed.

## Definitions

- **Encryption**: The conversion of data into a form that cannot be easily understood by unauthorized people
- **Encryption Key**: A string of characters that, used with a cryptographic algorithm, determines the specific operation to render information unreadable
- **Key Management System (KMS)**: A system for the management of cryptographic keys and their metadata throughout their lifecycle
- **Hardware Security Module (HSM)**: A physical computing device that safeguards and manages digital keys and performs cryptographic operations
