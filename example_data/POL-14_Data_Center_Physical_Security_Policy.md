# Data Center Physical Security Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines the requirements for establishing physical location and protection controls at data center facilities to reduce risks from environmental threats, hazards, and opportunities for unauthorized access.

## Scope

This policy applies to all data centers operated by or on behalf of the Organization.

## Policy

### Location

**Facility Address Confidentiality** — The physical address of every data center is classified as confidential and SHALL NOT be disclosed to those without a demonstrable need to know.

**Environmental Threat Reduction** — All new data centers SHOULD be located in areas unlikely to experience natural disasters, serious man-made accidents, or civil unrest. There SHALL be suitable distance between data centers such that a single environmental event would not impact more than one.

### Access Control

**Entry Monitoring** — Video cameras or other access control mechanisms that continuously monitor entry and exit points to secure areas SHALL be in place in all dedicated data center facilities. Images SHALL be retained for at least 90 days.

### Facility Structure

**Wall Construction** — Walls surrounding computer facilities SHALL be constructed of non-combustible material and resistant to fire for at least one hour. All openings to these walls SHALL be self-closing and fire-resistant for at least one hour.

**Windows** — There SHALL be no external windows in data center facilities. Where external windows cannot be avoided, they SHALL be reinforced with impact-rated glass or protective film and bars to prevent unauthorized access.

**Wall Extent** — Walls SHALL extend from true floor to true ceiling, or a secure data center cage may be used to ensure access can only be gained via access-controlled doors.

**Separation** — Servers and communications facilities SHALL be separated from the building's exterior wall by an additional internal wall.

### Intrusion Protection

**Security Perimeter** — Access to every secure area SHALL be controlled by a clearly defined security perimeter. Anti-tailgating mechanisms SHALL be in place at entry points.

**Guarded Access** — All third-party access to data center facilities containing sensitive information SHALL be controlled by guards, receptionists, or other staff.

**Intrusion Alarms** — All data centers SHALL be equipped with physical intrusion alarm systems that automatically alert those who can take immediate action.

**Secondary Doors** — All secondary data center doors SHALL be equipped with crash bars for emergency exit that also trigger a loud alarm when opened.

**Door Security** — Information processing facility rooms SHALL be equipped with fire doors and other doors resistant to forcible entry. Doors SHALL automatically close after opening and trigger an audible alarm when kept open beyond a brief period.

### Environmental Controls

**Alarm Systems** — All data centers SHALL be equipped with fire, water, and physical intrusion alarm systems that automatically alert those who can take immediate action.

**Fire Protection** — Data centers SHALL have fire detection and suppression systems, power conditioning, air conditioning, humidity control, and other environmental protection systems, adequately maintained. Above-floor sprinkler systems SHALL be dry pipe.

**Water and Flood Protection** — All locations housing computer and communications equipment SHALL meet minimum water damage prevention requirements, including being above ground level and flood levels, having adequate drainage, and not being situated below water tanks or pipes.

**Surge Protection** — Where weather and building conditions pose a significant risk of static electricity, equipment SHALL be outfitted with approved static protection equipment.

**Backup Utilities** — All new data centers SHALL be located with ready access to redundant electrical power and telecommunications. Redundant paths SHOULD be across separate areas where feasible.

**Power Backup Systems** — Facilities SHOULD include a power distribution system with utility feed, power management modules, reserve UPS, and backup fuel generators. Automatic transfer switches SHALL provide uninterrupted power supply.

### Cable Security

**Cabling Installation** — Installation and maintenance of power cables and telecommunication lines SHALL follow current industry security standards.

**Underground Placement** — Power and telecommunications cabling SHALL be underground where possible, or subject to adequate alternative protection.

**Routing and Conduits** — Network cabling SHALL be protected from unauthorized interception or damage by using conduit or avoiding routes through public areas.

**Segregation** — Power cables SHALL be segregated from communications cables to prevent interference.

**Labeling** — Clearly identifiable cable and equipment markings SHALL be used to minimize handling errors.

**Termination Points** — Telecommunications cabling SHALL be protected using armored conduit and locked rooms or boxes at inspection and termination points.

**Electromagnetic Shielding** — Telecommunications cabling SHALL be protected using electromagnetic shielding.

**Inspections** — Periodic technical sweeps and physical inspections SHALL be conducted to detect unauthorized devices attached to cables.

### Equipment Maintenance

**Equipment Maintenance** — All information systems equipment used for production processing SHOULD be maintained in accordance with supplier-recommended service intervals and specifications, with repairs performed only by qualified and authorized personnel.

**On-Site Repairs** — All equipment containing sensitive data SHALL be repaired within the Organization's physical premises. Equipment SHALL NOT be sent externally unless all sensitive data has been removed or the vendor has been specifically approved by the Information Security Department.

### Equipment Delivery and Removal

**Off-Site Equipment Approval** — Management SHALL authorize the use of any equipment outside of premises.

**Data Center Deliveries** — A secured intermediate holding area SHALL be used for computer supplies, equipment, and other deliveries.

## Definitions

- **Data Center Facility**: Any physical location where sensitive information is created, processed, or stored using computer and communications systems
- **Dry Pipe Sprinkler**: A fire suppression system in which pipes are filled with pressurized air rather than water, reducing the risk of accidental water damage to equipment
