# Generative AI Usage Policy

## Purpose

This policy outlines the Organization's commitment to responsible implementation of AI technology, ensuring that its use is consistent with organizational values, business standards, and security policies, and that associated risks are appropriately managed.

## Scope

This policy applies to all employees and contractors. It covers the use of all AI models and services, including but not limited to generative AI (GenAI) tools such as large language models, image generators, and code-generation assistants.

## Policy

### Acceptable Use

**Professional Conduct** — Employees SHALL use AI in a respectful and professional manner, refraining from using discriminatory language or any form of communication that could be perceived as offensive.

**Data Protection** — When utilizing AI, employees SHALL adhere to data protection policies. Data shared with third-party AI tools may be publicly accessible, potentially breaching regulations or resulting in intellectual property loss.

**Third-Party Exposure Assessment** — Employees SHALL NOT expose any organizational information to a third-party AI system without first establishing: whether data will be retained by the operator for training, who will have legal ownership of the data, who owns the responses, and who is legally liable if responses violate copyright or other laws.

**Legal and Regulatory Compliance** — Employees SHALL adhere to international laws, local laws, and organizational guidelines regarding intellectual property rights. The use of AI tools SHALL respect content originality and avoid intellectual property infringements.

**Human Review Requirement** — Output generated by AI tools SHALL NOT be incorporated into work products or shared with customers or external third parties without appropriate human review. There SHALL always be a human involved in the review and oversight process where the Organization bears any risk exposure.

### Prohibited Data Exposure

Employees SHALL NOT expose the following types of information to any third-party AI without completing security and architectural review and receiving written confirmation:

**Personal Data** — Information that can identify an individual, including name, address, identification numbers, date of birth, or health information.

**Confidential Information** — Non-public business plans, customer or partner information, sales data, financial information, product plans, technical information, passwords, and encryption keys.

**Company Code** — Proprietary code constituting trade secrets SHALL NOT be uploaded to third-party AI services. AI-generated code MUST NOT be incorporated into the Organization's software without review and approval by at least two members of the Engineering team.

**Customer Data** — Customer data SHALL NOT be exposed to third-party AI services. Information that can identify a customer SHALL NOT be used with AI tools.

### Implementation and Integration Guidelines

**Risk Assessment** — The Organization SHALL conduct appropriate risk assessments to identify and manage potential risks associated with AI technology.

**Attack Surface Mapping** — When integrating AI, teams SHALL explicitly map the attack surface available to attackers who can supply AI input or consume AI output.

**Logging** — All AI usage SHALL be logged and archived according to applicable laws and regulatory requirements. All access and actions taken by AI SHALL be logged and easily searchable.

**Minimal Access** — AI applications and integrations SHALL be provided with the minimal access, data, and permissions necessary to perform their tasks.

**Prompt Injection Awareness** — Teams SHALL always assume that AI may be compromised through prompt injection and plan mitigations and controls accordingly.

**Output Validation** — Output from AI systems SHALL be validated for accuracy and checked for fabricated answers or citations.

**Architectural Review** — Use of AI in any production system is subject to Architectural Review, and no work may be undertaken without following that process.

**Deepfake Awareness** — Employees SHOULD be aware that any content they encounter — images, recorded voice, or text — may be AI-generated.

### Personal Use Restrictions

**Licensed Tools** — Staff SHALL use Organization-licensed AI tools that have appropriate legal and technological protections for organizational data.

**Personal Cloud Services** — Personal AI service accounts SHALL NOT be used for storage, manipulation, or exchange of company-related data.

**Customer Data in AI** — Customer data SHALL NOT leave the production environment without customer permission. Where customer data needs processing by an AI system, that system SHOULD operate within a production context.

## Definitions

- **Generative AI (GenAI)**: AI technologies that create new content — text, images, code, or other media — based on patterns learned from training data
- **Large Language Model (LLM)**: A machine learning model trained on large text datasets that generates human-like text responses
- **Prompt Injection**: An attack technique where malicious input is crafted to manipulate an AI system into performing unintended actions
