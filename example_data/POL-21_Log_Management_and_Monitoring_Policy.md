# Log Management and Monitoring Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines the requirements for managing and monitoring the logs generated by the Organization's computer and communications systems, ensuring operational security and incident detection.

## Scope

This policy applies to all computer and communications systems of the Organization. The target audience is Information Technology employees and partners.

## Policy

### Audit Log Planning

**Log Management Infrastructure** — Management SHALL provide the budget and tools necessary to support a log management infrastructure.

**System Classification Requirements** — The Information Security Department SHALL establish specific logging requirements appropriate for the classification of the system being logged.

### Audit Logging Requirements

**System Audit Logs** — Logs of system security-relevant events SHALL provide sufficient data to support comprehensive audits on the effectiveness of and compliance with security measures.

**Communication Logging** — All electronic communications across computer systems and networks, including electronic mail and chat, SHOULD be recorded and logged.

**Application Security Logs** — All production application systems that handle security-relevant information SHOULD generate logs capturing every addition, modification, and deletion of sensitive data.

**Physical Access Logging** — The Organization SHALL maintain logs of all physical access to protected areas to the extent that access control systems are in place.

**Privileged Command Accountability** — All privileged commands issued by system administrators SHALL be traceable to specific individuals through comprehensive logs.

**Log Disclosure Restriction** — Systems logs and audit trails SHALL NOT be disclosed to any person outside the team of individuals who ordinarily view such information for their job duties or security incident investigations.

**Privileged Utilities** — Privileged utilities SHALL log user logins and activity such that users cannot easily tamper with those records. Privileged utilities SHOULD log into SIEM where technically feasible.

**Log Reliability** — Security logging systems SHALL be monitored for reliable operation. Periods when logging systems are unresponsive SHALL be recorded.

**Log Failure Alerts** — Alerts SHOULD be generated when logging systems malfunction, with severities commensurate with the risk.

### Audit Log Contents

**Minimum Log Contents** — All production systems SHALL have logs that record, at minimum: application start and stop times, system boot and restart times, and system configuration changes.

**Session Activity** — All systems running production applications SHOULD include logs recording user session activity including user IDs, access date and time, changes to user privileges, and system start-ups and shut-downs.

### Log Content Restrictions and Data Privacy

**Private Information** — Information systems SHALL be configured so that they do not capture private information in audit trails or logs.

**Password Logging Prohibition** — Unencrypted passwords, whether correctly typed or not, SHALL NEVER be recorded in system logs.

**Log Security** — Logging facilities and log information SHALL be protected against tampering and unauthorized access.

### Log Analysis and Reporting

**Log Viewing** — All event logs SHALL be made available in a human-readable format to facilitate analysis.

**Log Monitoring** — Processes for monitoring and reviewing logs and alerts SHALL be established to detect and respond to anomalous events and activities.

**Log Reporting** — The Organization SHALL implement tools necessary to report on events found within event logs.

**Log Normalization** — All log data SHALL be normalized to a consistent time and date format.

**SIEM** — The Organization SHALL employ security information and event management (SIEM) software to facilitate the collection, storage, and analysis of system audit logs.

### Management

**Log Retention** — Critical logs and audit trails SHOULD be retained for at least one year, taking cost and practicality into consideration.

### Clock Synchronization

**Accurate Clocks** — All computers connected to the internal network SHALL have the current time accurately reflected in their internal clocks.

**Time Protocol** — Time settings on all system components SHALL be kept current using Network Time Protocol (NTP) or similar technology.

**External Time Sources** — Specific external hosts SHALL be designated from which timeservers accept time updates.

## Definitions

- **Audit Trail**: A record showing who accessed an IT system and what operations were performed during a given period
- **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)**: Software that facilitates real-time collection, storage, and analysis of security events from multiple sources
- **Log Normalization**: Conversion of log data fields to a consistent representation and categorization
