# Acceptable Use Policy

## Purpose

This policy establishes the rules for acceptable usage of IT equipment, access management, data security, electronic communications, internet usage, physical security, and privacy within the Organization.

## Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties who access the Organization's information systems, networks, or facilities.

## Policy

### General Acceptable Use

IT systems, including hardware, software, operating systems, storage media, and SaaS services, are the property of the Organization and SHALL be used for business purposes. Data created on corporate systems remains the property of the Organization.

Personnel MUST NOT engage in any activity that is illegal under applicable law while utilizing Organization-owned resources, including but not limited to:

- Violating intellectual property rights or installing unlicensed software
- Introducing malicious programs into the network (viruses, worms, trojans)
- Circumventing user authentication or security controls
- Storing confidential information on unsanctioned cloud services or unencrypted removable media
- Providing employee information to unauthorized external parties

### IT Equipment

All equipment issued by the Organization is primarily for business use. Personnel SHOULD limit non-business use. Only Organization-managed systems SHALL be connected to the internal network. Equipment MUST be returned to IT upon upgrade or separation from the Organization.

### Access Management

Access to information SHALL be granted based on "need to know" and "least privilege" principles. All remote access MUST use approved corporate VPNs. Users are responsible for all activity performed with their credentials and MUST NOT share user IDs, passwords, or other authentication credentials with any other person. User access SHALL be provisioned through a formal request process, periodically reviewed, and promptly revoked upon role change or termination.

### Passwords

Users SHALL comply with strong password requirements and MUST NOT choose dictionary words, personal details, or common sequences. Passwords MUST NOT be written down or stored near access devices. Employees SHALL use the Organization-provided password management tool. All workstations SHALL be secured with password-protected lock screens activating after 15 minutes or less of inactivity.

### Data Security

Corporate information is classified into three categories: confidential, internal, and public. Information MUST be handled, transferred, and destroyed according to its classification. Only authorized cloud applications SHALL be used for sharing confidential or internal information. Encrypted communication MUST be used when transmitting confidential information over public networks.

### Email Security

Electronic communication resources are for corporate business objectives. Auto-forwarding messages outside corporate systems is prohibited. Personnel MUST NOT use personal email accounts to send or receive confidential information. Employees SHALL exercise caution when opening attachments from unknown senders.

### Internet Usage

Internet access is provided for business purposes. Personal use SHALL be limited to occasional access. Access to websites and downloadable content SHALL be restricted through URL and content filtering. Network services MUST NOT be used to create, send, or store content that constitutes harassment or disparagement.

### Physical Security

Personnel MUST display photo ID access cards at all times while in facilities. Piggy-backing, tailgating, and door propping are prohibited. Visitors in access-controlled areas MUST be accompanied by authorized personnel.

### Clear Desk and Clear Screen

The Organization operates a clear desk and clear screen policy. Personnel SHALL lock workstations when leaving their workspace. Confidential information MUST be secured in locked storage when the workstation is unattended. Passwords MUST NOT be posted on or under computers.

### Privacy

Information created, sent, or stored on the Organization's resources is not private and may be accessed by authorized IT personnel. Systems administrators may monitor equipment, systems, and network traffic to resolve technical issues, perform audits, or investigate security incidents.
